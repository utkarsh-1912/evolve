Dataset Architecture, Privacy-Preserving Technical Stack, and Multi-Agent Orchestration for the Abu Dhabi Inherited Disease Risk Challenge
Solving the Abu Dhabi Future Health Digital Innovation Challenge for [Fertility] Understanding and Reducing Inherited Disease Risk requires bridging the gap between population-scale whole-genome sequencing and culturally trusted, privacy-preserving family decision support1. While the Department of Health – Abu Dhabi (DoH), the Abu Dhabi Public Health Centre (ADPHC), and M42 have established a mandatory pre-marital genetic screening foundation analyzing 570 genes linked to more than 840 autosomal recessive conditions via 30X Whole-Genome Sequencing (WGS)2, screening only achieves clinical impact when individuals and couples comprehend probabilistic carrier findings, navigate consanguinity without stigma or coercion, and connect seamlessly to accredited genetic counseling and reproductive care1. Across 11,748 couples screened between the 2022 pilot launch and July 2026, the program identified 565 genetically incompatible couples (~4.8% overall operational positivity, reported as approximately 5%, with 11% to 14% positivity observed in earlier enriched cohorts)2. However, translating these variant calls into informed family choices faces three structural bottlenecks: 52% of pathogenic and likely pathogenic (P/LP) variants identified in Emirati families are absent from gnomAD and 30% are absent from ClinVar8; naive sharing of carrier reports risks exposing discordant individual variants across partners or extended relatives without consent1; and raw genomic pipelines do not natively account for complex consanguineous pedigrees or assay-specific residual risks9.
To address these requirements, this report delivers a production-grade specification across three core pillars:
Multi-Modal Dataset & Synthetic Benchmark Architecture: A four-layer data foundation combining Emirati population allele frequencies (CYP21A2 at 10.6% carrier frequency, HBB at 9.6%, MEFV at 5.9%, ABCA4 at 4.3%, HBA2 at 1.8%, SMN1 at 1.3%, DONSON at 1.0%, and ALDH3A2 at 0.6%)8, HL7 FHIR R4 Genomics Reporting Implementation Guide (IG) profiles12, GA4GH Phenopackets v2 and GA4GH Pedigree schemas14, deterministic Wright's path-coefficient () and Bayesian residual risk models17, and a bilingual (Emirati Arabic/English) counseling and psychometric evaluation corpus (GCOS-24, MMIC, and DCS/SURE)20.
Sovereign Privacy-Preserving Technical Architecture: A six-tier stack compliant with the Abu Dhabi Healthcare Information and Cyber Security Standard (ADHICS) and UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 202123. The architecture introduces Authorized Private Set Intersection (APSI) so prospective partners can verify mutual recessive compatibility across the 570-gene panel while mathematically hiding non-overlapping personal carrier states25, governed by SMART on FHIR v2 OAuth 2.0 scopes and HL7 FHIR Consent resources integrated with Malaffi (Abu Dhabi's Health Information Exchange) and SEHA clinical pathways4.
Neuro-Symbolic Multi-Agent System & Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Topology: A seven-agent orchestration pattern that separates deterministic mathematical and bioinformatic computation from generative bilingual communication. A constitutional Bioethics & Legal Guardrail Supervisor enforces non-directive counseling, UAE statutory compliance (Federal Law No. 7 of 2019 on Medically Assisted Reproduction, Federal Law No. 11 of 2008 prohibiting third-party gamete donation, and Cabinet Resolution No. 44 of 2024 on fetal anomaly regulations)29, and mandatory escalation of positive, ambiguous, or high-distress cases to Abu Dhabi's >100 genomics-trained physicians and accredited genetic counselors1.
1. Epidemiological Baseline and Ecosystem Context in Abu Dhabi
Designing an effective digital and multi-agent solution requires grounding every data model and clinical workflow in the empirical realities of the Emirati Genome Programme (EGP) and the Abu Dhabi Pre-Marital Genetic Screening Programme3. Overseen by the UAE Genome Council in partnership with DoH Abu Dhabi, ADPHC, M42, and Khalifa University, the EGP has collected over 750,000 genomic samples toward its one-million-sample national target and constructed the consensus Emirati Reference Genome Platform (ERGP) alongside the UAE Healthy Future Study (UAEHFS) of 15,000 Emirati nationals3. Building on a pilot initiated in 2022, DoH Abu Dhabi integrated mandatory pre-marital genetic testing for all Emirati citizens effective October 1, 2024, followed by a federal mandate issued by the Ministry of Health and Prevention (MoHAP) effective January 20256.

A close examination of the published clinical and operational data resolves apparent differences in reported couple incompatibility rates across different stages of the UAE rollout2. In the 2022 pilot (>800 couples) and the landmark study published in the New England Journal of Medicine (Al Ameri et al., July 2026, NEJMc2510402, 1,094 couples), couple-level genetic risk was identified in 14.0% and 13.1% (143/1,094) of couples, respectively6. Similarly, M42's bioinformatics validation on >1,000 early couples reported an ~11% positive rate, whereas 1,000 sex-matched random-mating permutation simulations predicted a baseline of 8.5%2. As the NEJM authors explicitly noted, early voluntary cohorts were enriched for couples with a known family history of genetic disorders7. As mandatory screening scaled across the broader population to 11,748 couples through July 2026, the operational incompatibility rate converged to 4.81% (565 couples, or ~5%)5, aligning with the 4% to 21% at-risk range modeled by Rabea et al. across varying carrier panel definitions8.
At the bioinformatics layer, the M42 automated framework processes 30X WGS variant call sets (SNPs, Indels, and CNVs) across the 570 target genes, automatically clearing and reporting 88.2% of couples directly as negative and isolating the remaining ~11.8% for manual clinical curation and confirmatory workups2. End-to-end result turnaround takes up to 14 calendar days under DoH Abu Dhabi guidance (and up to 17 working days under Emirates Health Services guidelines for UAE citizens, compared to 5 working days for standard non-genetic premarital bloodwork)35.
Crucially, in the NEJM cohort, DoH Abu Dhabi issued marriage health certificates and transmitted them to the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department regardless of whether a couple was found to be genetically at risk7. Following non-directive genetic counseling, approximately 33% of at-risk couples voluntarily chose not to proceed with marriage, while the remaining ~67% proceeded with marriage and were referred for reproductive counseling and Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART), including in vitro fertilization (IVF) with Pre-implantation Genetic Testing for Monogenic disorders (PGT-M)5. This empirical outcome directly informs the challenge's core evaluation constraint: a digital innovation solution must measure informed comprehension, counseling uptake, and timely care navigation, and must never treat marriage cancellation or any single personal decision as its success metric1.
2. Deep Research on Dataset Architecture & Synthetic Benchmark Design
Because live genomic sequences from the EGP and patient records within Malaffi are strictly protected health information5, developing, validating, and benchmarking a solution for the challenge requires a Four-Layer Dataset Architecture. This architecture pairs public reference databases with empirically calibrated synthetic datasets that mirror the exact allele frequencies, structural variant complexities, consanguinity loops, and clinical pathways of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi5.

2.2 Layer 2: Interoperable Clinical & Genomic Data Schemas (HL7 FHIR + GA4GH)
To integrate with Malaffi and hospital Electronic Health Records (EHRs) across SEHA and private providers5, the dataset avoids custom JSON structures and implements two harmonized international standards: HL7 FHIR R4 Genomics Reporting IG (hl7.fhir.uv.genomics-reporting STU3 / v4.0.0-cibuild) and GA4GH Phenopackets v2 / Pedigree13.
Within the HL7 FHIR R4 Genomics Reporting framework, three core profiles form the structural backbone of every screening record13. First, the GenomicReport (DiagnosticReport profile) encapsulates the screening event, referencing the tested Specimen (requiring Specimen.type and Specimen.receivedTime), the GenomicStudy methodology, and top-level Observation resources via DiagnosticReport.result12. To prevent duplicate traversal paths that break downstream clinical decision support, child observations linked via hasMember must not be redundantly nested inside DiagnosticReport.result13. Reports also include a version identifier and set DiagnosticReport.status = amended when reissued under updated curation guidance12. Second, Variant and Genotype (Observation profiles) map each VCF variant call using mandatory components: component:gene-studied populated with numeric HGNC IDs rather than gene symbol strings alone, component:genomic-ref-seq (NCBI RefSeq or approved Locus Reference Genomic LRG sequence), component:coordinate-system, component:exact-start-end, component:ref-allele, component:alt-allele, component:genomic-hgvs (valid HGVS nomenclature), and allelic state encoded with GENO ontology terms (GENO:0000135 for heterozygous, GENO:0000134 for homozygous/hemizygous)12. Third, the DiagnosticImplication (Observation profile) references the underlying Variant or Genotype via Observation.derivedFrom12. Following the HL7 Genomics Reporting IG, the term "carrier" is used only for autosomal recessive, X-linked recessive, or incomplete penetrance conditions, with Observation.note stating "this patient is at least a carrier" for autosomal recessive pathogenic variants12. Where confirmatory parental or orthogonal testing is needed, DiagnosticReport.extension:recommended-action references a FHIR Task12. For couple-level reporting, the CodeX GenomeX Data Exchange (Prenatal Carrier Screening) profile nests individual partner observations (Observation/PrenatalVariant...PatientFemale and PatientMale) into a dependent overarching report48.
Complementing FHIR's transactional structure, GA4GH Phenopackets v2 (published as an ISO standard) and the GA4GH Pedigree standard provide computable representations of phenotypic and family health history data14. Each Phenopacket links an Individual to PhenotypicFeature elements encoded with Human Phenotype Ontology (HPO) terms, capturing both observed clinical findings and explicitly excluded abnormalities (e.g., excluding a morphological defect via normal testing) alongside onset (TimeElement) and severity modifiers14. Quantitative premarital laboratory assays are stored in Measurement elements with mandatory units and normal reference ranges14, while Interpretation blocks encode genomic variations using the GA4GH Variation Representation Specification (vrs-protobuf) and diseases via the Mondo Disease Ontology16. All Phenopackets are validated with phenopacket-tools to verify metadata resources and enforce the ontology annotation propagation rule (a phenopacket must not contain both a term and its ancestor)16. Multi-generational family trees and consanguinity loops are represented using the GA4GH Pedigree standard, which interoperates directly with both Phenopackets and HL7 FHIR15.

2.5 Layer 4: Cultural, Regulatory, and Psychometric Evaluation Corpus
The fourth dataset layer comprises a bilingual knowledge base (Arabic and English) alongside standardized psychometric evaluation instruments to benchmark the system's Desired Outcomes without using cancelled marriages as a success metric1. Rather than relying on ad-hoc satisfaction surveys, the evaluation corpus embeds three validated clinical genetics instruments20:
Genetic Counseling Outcome Scale (GCOS-24) & Genomics Outcome Scale (GOS): A 24-item patient-reported outcome measure validated for clinical genetics services (internal consistency , test-retest reliability ), paired with its short form (GOS), measuring cognitive and decisional empowerment pre- and post-counseling20.
Multidimensional Measure of Informed Choice (MMIC): Evaluates whether a user's reproductive screening or care choice reflects both adequate objective knowledge (comprehension of carrier status, inheritance probability, and residual risk) and congruence with their personal attitude and values1.
Decisional Conflict Scale (DCS) & 4-Item SURE Screening Test: Assesses contributing factors such as feeling informed, clarity of personal values, and perceived support21. Any user indicating unresolved conflict on the SURE test is automatically routed to a human genetic counselor1.
3. Sovereign Privacy-Preserving Technical Architecture
To satisfy the challenge's mandate for privacy-preserving family workflows, explicit consent, role-based access, data minimization, and auditable sharing within Abu Dhabi's regulatory environment1, the technical architecture is structured into six sovereign tiers. All storage, compute, and cryptographic operations reside within UAE-local infrastructure governed by the Abu Dhabi Healthcare Information and Cyber Security Standard (ADHICS) and UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021 (Personal Data Protection Law)23.

3.1 Tier 1 & Tier 2: Sovereign Genomic Ingestion and FHIR/GA4GH Standardization
When a citizen books a premarital or preconception screening appointment via the SEHA application or by calling 80050 at one of the 22 designated healthcare centers and hospitals across Abu Dhabi (Baniyas, Madinat Khalifa, Al Shamkha, Al Maqtaa, Al Bateen, Al Bahia), Al Ain (Al Muwaiji, Al Maqam, Oud Al Touba Diagnostic and Screening Center, Neima, Al Hayer, Al Quaa, Mezyad, Al Yahar, Sweihan, Al Hili), or Al Dhafra (Al Dhafra Family Medicine Center, Ghiathy Hospital, Marfa Hospital, Al Sila Hospital, Delma Hospital, Liwa Hospital)4, blood or saliva samples are sequenced at 30X WGS coverage2. The M42 pipeline processes SNPs, Indels, and CNVs across the 570 target genes using ClinVar and HGMD annotations2. A transformation service then serializes each patient's calls into an HL7 FHIR R4 GenomicReport Bundle (hl7.fhir.uv.genomics-reporting) and a GA4GH Phenopacket v2 object13. Every bundle is validated using the HL7 FHIR Validator CLI (java -jar validator_cli.jar Bundle-example.json -ig hl7.fhir.uv.genomics-reporting) with cached hl7.terminology.r4 and hl7.fhir.uv.extensions.r4 packages, alongside automated unit tests that traverse all hasMember and derivedFrom chains to guarantee zero dangling references13.
3.2 Tier 3: Cryptographic Privacy Enclave — Authorized Private Set Intersection (APSI)
A central ethical and technical dilemma in premarital and family carrier screening is asymmetric information exposure1. In comprehensive genome-sequencing carrier studies, up to 78% of tested individuals carry at least one recessive variant across broad panels62, whereas only 4.8% to 13.1% of couples share pathogenic variants in the same recessive gene5. If Partner A's individual carrier status for a non-overlapping condition (e.g., GJB2 hearing loss) is disclosed to Partner B or Partner B's family during a compatibility check where Partner B does not carry a GJB2 variant, Partner A may experience unnecessary anxiety, social stigma, or family tension despite the couple having no shared recessive compatibility issue for that locus1.
To prevent unauthorized cross-partner disclosure mathematically, Tier 3 implements Authorized Private Set Intersection (APSI) and Private Set Intersection Cardinality (PSI-CA) protocols25:
Authority-Signed Carrier Sets: To prevent malicious clients from fabricating arbitrary gene sets to probe a partner's genome, the DoH/M42 FHIR server acts as the certifying authority under the Authorized PSI (APSI) model introduced by De Cristofaro and Tsudik26. For Partner A, the server encodes the set of autosomal recessive genes (encoded as numeric HGNC integer IDs) in which Partner A carries a P/LP variant, , signs each element with the server's RSA private key, and inserts the signed elements into a Bloom filter13.
Blinded Intersection Computation: When Partner A and Partner B mutually consent to a compatibility evaluation, the client generates a random blinding factor and modular inverse for each element in Partner B's signed gene set  and evaluates the intersection against the Bloom filter in a third-party / server-assisted PSI setting56.
Zero-Leakage Output Enforcement: When  (the ~88.2%–95.2% compatible majority)2, the protocol outputs an empty intersection (Compatible / No Shared Recessive Locus), restricting either party from learning any other information about the partner's private discordant variants56. Each individual still accesses their own private carrier findings and educational material inside their personal health portal1. Conversely, when  (the ~4.8%–11.8% incompatible or manual-review cohort)2, the protocol outputs only the intersecting gene set  into the mutually consented Couple Counseling Workspace, immediately initiating confirmatory assay verification and genetic counseling referral1.
Privacy-Preserving Family Cascade Referrals: Because carrier screening for extended family members is vital when a severe mutation (such as CYP1B1, CYP21A2, or SMN1) is identified in an index case8, the system allows the index patient to generate an auditable, single-locus Cascade Screening Token referencing the target HGNC ID and GA4GH VRS variant digest1. Relatives can take this token to a SEHA screening clinic for targeted carrier testing without exposing the index patient's identity or full genomic report1.

3.4 Tier 6: Closed-Loop Ecosystem Integration (Malaffi, SEHA, Fertility Centers, Judicial Link)
Because the HL7 Genomics Reporting IG defines data structures rather than workflow routing13, Tier 6 implements a Screening-to-Care Orchestration Bus across Malaffi1. First, in accordance with DoH Abu Dhabi policy documented in the NEJM study, once premarital screening and counseling steps are recorded, the health certificate is issued and transmitted to the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department regardless of genetic test results or at-risk status7. Second, every actionable screening finding is tracked through a closed-loop state machine (Result Issued  Patient Notified  Acknowledgement & Comprehension Confirmed  Confirmatory Testing Completed  Genetic Counseling Completed  Reproductive Medicine / PGT-M Referral Completed  Documented Human Closure), ensuring clear clinical ownership at every transition across laboratories, primary care centers, and specialist fertility clinics1.
4. Neuro-Symbolic Multi-Agent Architecture and Role Specifications
A single monolithic Large Language Model (LLM) is ill-suited for clinical genetics decision support: unconstrained generative models can hallucinate conditional probabilities during Bayesian calculations, leak sensitive context across partners if session memory is shared, or drift into directive advice1. To eliminate these failure modes, the system employs a Seven-Agent Neuro-Symbolic Architecture where deterministic symbolic tools execute all mathematical, bioinformatic, and consent-verification operations, while generative agents handle empathetic bilingual communication under the strict supervision of an inline bioethics guardrail gate.

4.1 Detailed Agent Role Specifications, Tool Bindings, and Guardrails
Agent 1: Privacy & Consent Guardian Agent (ConsentGuardian)
ConsentGuardian serves as the zero-trust security gateway between individual users, prospective partners, extended family members, and clinical staff1. Bound to the SMART on FHIR v2 OAuth 2.0 authorization server, the HL7 FHIR Consent registry, and the APSI cryptographic service26, it verifies that every session token is strictly scoped to a single authenticated patient/ context27. When Partner A requests a couple compatibility check with Partner B, ConsentGuardian dispatches an explicit consent request to Partner B, invokes the APSI enclave only after both Consent resources are cryptographically signed, and logs every access event to an immutable Malaffi audit trail1. If either user revokes consent at any stage, ConsentGuardian immediately terminates the shared workspace token and purges cached couple state1.
Agent 2: Genomic Variant & Confirmatory Assay Interpreter Agent (VariantInterpreter)
VariantInterpreter deterministically parses HL7 FHIR GenomicReport bundles across the 570-gene panel, querying ClinVar, HGMD, gnomAD v4.1 (MID), and the Emirati Reference Genome Platform (ERGP)2. It extracts numeric HGNC gene IDs, RefSeq/LRG transcripts, HGVS nomenclature, and zygosity (GENO:0000135 / GENO:0000134)12, while checking local Emirati curation tables to capture the 52% of Emirati P/LP variants absent from gnomAD and 30% absent from ClinVar8. Crucially, VariantInterpreter evaluates locus-specific technical limitations: if a variant or discordant call involves a technically complex gene such as CYP21A2 or copy-number screening in SMN17, the agent attaches a DiagnosticReport.extension:recommended-action pointing to an actionable FHIR Task for orthogonal confirmatory testing or parental carrier confirmation prior to prenatal or reproductive decision-making7.
Agent 3: Consanguinity Pedigree & Bayesian Quantitative Risk Agent (PedigreeRiskEngine)
PedigreeRiskEngine conducts structured 3–4 generation family history intake, constructs a computable GA4GH Pedigree and Phenopacket v2, and executes deterministic Python solvers for Wright's path coefficients () and Bayesian residual and reproductive risks14. During intake, it systematically queries family relationships (siblings, parents, grandparents, aunts, uncles, first and second cousins), grandparental ethnicity, congenital anomalies, early hearing or vision impairment, failure to thrive, intellectual disability or developmental delay, inherited blood disorders (such as thalassemia), seizure disorders, and unexplained neonatal or infant deaths52. It then calculates  and  across all pedigree loops (including the  ancestral inbreeding adjustment) and computes exact post-test Bayesian residual risks using Emirati gene carrier frequencies () and assay detection rates ()8. Operating as a pure symbolic agent, PedigreeRiskEngine locks all numerical outputs into immutable JSON fields so downstream generative agents never perform free-text arithmetic.
Agent 4: Culturally Sensitive Bilingual Risk Communicator Agent (CulturalCommunicator)
CulturalCommunicator translates the locked JSON risk outputs from VariantInterpreter and PedigreeRiskEngine into plain, non-stigmatizing Arabic and English narratives tailored to the user's literacy level1. Following established consanguinity counseling guidelines, the agent always presents both absolute and relative risks (for example, explaining that a "three-fold increase" corresponds to an absolute probability of roughly 1 in 40 rather than 1 in 120, or that >94% of children of first-cousin unions without a shared monogenic disorder are born unaffected)11. It clearly distinguishes asymptomatic carrier status from disease diagnosis, avoids framing consanguineous marriage as a "mistake," and uses an interactive teach-back micro-quiz aligned with the MMIC knowledge framework to confirm patient understanding before closing the communication loop1.
Agent 5: Values-Sensitive Reproductive Options & Care Navigator Agent (CareNavigator)
CareNavigator translates screening and counseling findings into timely, personalized, and non-directive care pathways across Abu Dhabi's healthcare network1. It connects users directly to SEHA's 22 premarital screening centers via the SEHA app or 800504, accredited genetic counseling clinics, and DOH-licensed Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART) centers offering IVF/ICSI with PGT-M (such as SEHA Reproductive Medicine and Trust Fertility at Burjeel Medical City)29. For Emirati citizens covered under Thiqa, CareNavigator evaluates readiness against the DoH ART Clinical Practice Guideline (2023) and Thiqa Coverage Policy55:
Immediate Eligibility Verification: Confirms that an abnormal pre-marital screening result, genetic carrier status, or family history of genetic conditions qualifies the couple to initiate diagnostic and reproductive evaluation immediately without waiting for 12 months of unassisted conception attempts55.
Maternal Age & Ovarian Reserve Guidance: Checks that the female partner falls within the preferred 18–45 age range (or up to under 48 years under Thiqa if aged 46–47 with an expert-assessed Antral Follicle Count ≥5; ART is strongly discouraged over age 47)55.
Metabolic & BMI Pre-Optimization: Checks whether maternal BMI falls within the preferred 19–35 kg/m² range and Thiqa's 19–40 kg/m² eligibility window; for women with a BMI between 35 and 40 kg/m², the agent provides clear information on treatment and obstetric risks and offers a direct referral to a registered dietitian for a minimum 3-month weight management intervention alongside genetic counseling55.
Cryopreservation & Prenatal Follow-Up: Informs eligible couples that Thiqa covers the first year of medically necessary oocyte and embryo cryopreservation as part of the bundled package, and reminds couples that per ASRM and DoH guidelines, confirmatory prenatal testing is still recommended during pregnancy following PGT-M due to technical embryo biopsy limitations29.
Agent 6: Clinical Safety, Cultural & Bioethical Guardrail Supervisor Agent (BioethicsSupervisor)
BioethicsSupervisor operates as a mandatory, inline constitutional firewall that inspects every draft message from CulturalCommunicator and CareNavigator before delivery to the user1. Any response that fails one of four deterministic verification rules is blocked and regenerated:
Zero Numerical Drift: Verifies that every probability, percentage, and odds ratio in the outbound text matches the locked JSON fields computed by PedigreeRiskEngine19.
Zero Cross-Partner Data Leakage: Verifies that no gene symbol, variant notation, or personal health history belonging to Partner A is present in a message addressed to Partner B unless that locus is part of a mutually consented compatibility intersection ()1.
Non-Coercion & Judicial Decoupling: Blocks any directive phrasing advising a couple to cancel or proceed with marriage, and ensures couples are accurately informed that DoH issues marriage health certificates to the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department regardless of genetic compatibility status1.
UAE Statutory Reproductive Law Compliance: Enforces strict compliance with UAE federal legislation. Specifically, it blocks any suggestion of third-party donor sperm, donor eggs, or donor embryos, which are prohibited across the UAE under Federal Law No. 11 of 200830; requires written consent of both spouses before any embryo undergoes PGT-M testing under Federal Law No. 7 of 2019 (noting that Federal Decree-Law No. 17 of 2023 updated gestational surrogacy provisions between spouses under DoH standards while preserving the prohibition on third-party donor gametes)29; and ensures any discussion of severe fetal abnormality during pregnancy strictly adheres to Article 16 of the UAE Medical Liability Law (as amended by Federal Decree-Law No. 18 of 2023) and Cabinet Resolution No. 44 of 2024 (which mandates a 120-day gestational limit for Resolution 44 cases, written consent of the pregnant woman, approval by the treating physician and a licensed OB/GYN at an authorized facility, and a 5-working-day multidisciplinary committee review), routing all prenatal anomaly inquiries immediately to a licensed maternal-fetal medicine physician and accredited genetic counselor31.
Agent 7: Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) Escalation & Counselor Copilot Agent (CounselorCopilot)
CounselorCopilot preserves a strict boundary between digital decision support and clinical medical advice by routing complex, high-impact, or emotionally sensitive cases to Abu Dhabi's >100 genomics-trained Emirati doctors and accredited genetic counselors1. Escalation to a human professional is mandatory whenever: (a) a couple is identified as genetically incompatible ()5; (b) a discordant couple has an elevated post-test Bayesian residual carrier risk due to consanguinity or technical assay limits (CYP21A2 or SMN1)9; (c) a shared Variant of Uncertain Significance (VUS) is detected inside a large Run of Homozygosity (ROH > 10 Mb) or the 3–4 generation pedigree reveals congenital anomalies, intellectual disability, or unexplained infant death11; or (d) the user exhibits unresolved decisional conflict on the SURE screener or fails the teach-back comprehension check1. For the receiving clinician, CounselorCopilot pre-assembles a concise Malaffi Consultation Brief containing the GA4GH Pedigree graph, the FHIR GenomicReport variant summary, pending confirmatory Task orders, and the couple's documented questions and values1.
